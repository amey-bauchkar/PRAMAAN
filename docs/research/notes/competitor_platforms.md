# Competitor Platforms: Campus Placement / TPO and Early-Career Hiring (India and Global), as of Nov 2026

Scope note: These notes come from about 35 searches and page fetches made on 2026-11-17. Most sources are vendor marketing pages, vendor blogs or press releases. Few are help-centre documentation or independent reviews. Each claim is marked **[marketing]**, **[docs]**, **[press/3rd-party]** or **[candidate-report]** where that matters. Anything dated before 2024 is flagged **(pre-2024, possibly outdated)**. Several named platforms (Placewit, Campus Credentials as a software product, uConnect AI features, Turing's current vetting, Woven's current format, Coursera's credential standard) returned little or no usable evidence. They are listed under Gaps rather than guessed.

## Q1. What does each platform offer? (feature inventory)

### Takeaway
Indian TPO platforms (Superset, PrepInsta Optimus, iamneo NeoPAT ERP, CollPoll, Eklavvya, Mettl) cover the operational loop: student data, rule-based eligibility filters, drives, assessments, proctoring, AI mock interviews, and offers. Global assessment vendors (HackerRank, CodeSignal, HackerEarth, Karat, HireVue, Unstop, Talview, Glider, Eightfold) are moving fast toward autonomous AI interviewers and "agent" suites in 2025–2026. None of the sources describe evidence-anchored skill proofs from a student's own repositories, versioned eligibility rules with what-if or fairness preview, eligibility appeals, or college-issued W3C/Open Badges 3.0 skill credentials.

## Q2. Which platforms analyse candidates' own GitHub/code?

### Takeaway
None of the major assessment or TPO platforms evidently verify skills from a student's *own* repositories with code-level anchors. HackerRank Chakra, Karat NextGen and CodeSignal assess candidates on **vendor-supplied** repositories. GitHub-mining exists in recruiter-side sourcing startups (Prog.ai, Skillsync, GitHired, GitCruiter) and Mercor's matching. These infer skills for search; they do not produce student-owned, anchor-cited proofs.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- PRAMAAN (a) is not offered by any campus/TPO platform found. The closest analogues are recruiter-side GitHub sourcing tools, which (1) lack student consent and college ownership, (2) produce inferred profiles rather than anchored evidence, and (3) are not tied to eligibility or drives.
- Mercor is the most significant commercial player that reportedly ingests GitHub, but only as a matching signal.

### Gaps
- Mercor's GitHub use comes from a secondary source; Mercor's first-party documentation was not retrieved.
- Turing's vetting (historically included code challenges) is unverified for 2026.

## Q3. Which do "explain your code" / code-walkthrough interviews, or AI questions generated from the candidate's own work?

### Takeaway
"Explain your reasoning" probing is now mainstream on **vendor tasks**: HackerRank Chakra, Karat NextGen, and Byteboard's design-doc reasoning. AI questions generated from a candidate's **own repo** exist only in open-source and hackathon projects (Code Defense, GitReady, Project-to-Interview, BackendNeuron) and resume-based prep (Apna). None of the sources found combine "predict your own code's behaviour, then modify it under hidden tests" (PRAMAAN b).

### Cited Findings
- Chakra asks candidates "why they chose one approach over another" and how a solution would change under a new constraint — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/hackerranks-ai-interviewer-offers-a-glimpse-into-what-job-interviews-could-become/)
- Karat NextGen: Interview Engineers "probe their reasoning" on multi-file AI-assisted projects — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251210685922/en). Candidate guides describe accepting, amending or rejecting AI changes with tests [candidate-report] — [PracHub](https://prachub.com/resources/karat-nextgen-interview-review-ai-changes-in-a-multi-file-project-and-defend-your-decisions)
- Byteboard (now Karat): roughly 2-hour work sample with "a design-document reasoning exercise and a code implementation exercise" [competitor description] — [CodeSignal blog](https://codesignal.com/blog/top-byteboard-alternatives-for-technical-hiring-in-2026/)
- Code Defense (open source): paste a repo or PR/commit URL and answer "6 AI-generated questions" with AI feedback — [Code Defense](https://gh.r-mj.com/AsadullahSamo/Code-Defense)
- GitReady (IBM hackathon): clones a public repo and targets the "walk me through this code" question — [lablab.ai](https://lablab.ai/ai-hackathons/ibm-bob-hackathon/zayn/gitready)
- BackendNeuron AI Interviewer "analyzes a GitHub repository and asks technical questions" — [GitHub](https://github.com/BackendNeuron/ai-interviewer)
- Apna AI Job Prep personalises mock interviews from resumes — [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/news/apna-ai-job-prep-records-39-lakh-minutes-across-76-lakh-interview-simulations-9374546)
- Mercor's AI interviewer reportedly has "full context about a candidate's background" — [CO/AI](https://getcoai.com/news/how-mercor-uses-ai-to-conduct-interviews-and-match-candidates-with-jobs/)

### Inferences
- The "defend-your-code" concept is validated by industry, since HackerRank and Karat both score reasoning. PRAMAAN's novelty lies in (1) using the student's *own* verified code, and (2) the predict-then-modify-under-hidden-tests protocol, which resists AI-written portfolios. No commercial vendor was found doing (2) on candidate-owned code.
- Open-source repo-to-questions tools show that the question-generation part is easy to copy. The defensible part is anchoring, hidden-test mutation, and linking results to proof levels and eligibility.

### Gaps
- No access to Chakra's internal docs to confirm whether customers can upload their own repos (for example, a candidate's take-home).
- Glider AI and Talview Ivy question-generation sources (resume vs JD) are not documented in retrieved sources.

## Q4. Which offer eligibility rule engines with versioning, appeals, or fairness previews? (and fairness monitoring)

### Takeaway
Rule-based eligibility filtering (CGPA, backlogs, branch, skills, assessment thresholds) is standard in Indian TPO tools such as Superset. No platform was found documenting rule **versioning**, **what-if impact preview**, **four-fifths fairness preview before publishing**, or a student **appeals** workflow. Fairness monitoring exists globally only as periodic NYC LL144 bias audits (Eightfold, Harver) or as marketing ("bias monitoring", HireVue). These are not per-drive, per-funnel-stage previews for placement officers.

### Cited Findings
- Superset eligibility: configurable criteria, auto-refresh, "diversity preferences", students "see exactly which criteria they met or missed", officers can "justify inclusion or exclusion". No versioning, what-if or appeals [marketing blog, Oct 2025] — [Superset blog](https://joinsuperset.com/blogs/intelligent-eligibility-matching-using-a-placement-portal-to-align-student-profiles-with-recruiter-requirements/)
- Generic placement software guides describe per-drive CGPA, backlog and skill criteria with auto-generated eligible lists — [PlacementPreparation.io](https://www.placementpreparation.io/blog/best-placement-management-software-for-colleges/)
- An internal SOP template describes "Reevaluate students who improve eligibility; Update placement status" (manual requalification, not appeals software) — [Zollege SOP](https://knowledge.zollege.com/sop-internal/placement-support-eligibility-review)
- Eightfold LL144 audit (Mar 2026): impact ratio = group scoring rate ÷ highest group's rate; <0.80 = adverse impact under the four-fifths rule; Eightfold does not certify "bias-free" — [Eightfold audit](https://eightfold.ai/trust/bias-audit-results/)
- Harver 2025 LL144 audit — [Harver PDF](https://harver.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Harver-Platform-2025-Bias-Audit.pdf)
- HireVue AI Interviewer page lists "bias monitoring" and "explainability logs" [marketing] — [HireVue](https://hirevue.com/platform/ai-interviewer). A third party claims HireVue requires customers to build their own bias-audit pipeline (unverified) — [The Neural Base](https://theneuralbase.com/ai-for-hr/learn/advanced/hirevue-assessments/)
- Context: the Stanford study (May 2026) found adverse impact in pymetrics-scored jobs — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/26/ai-hiring-algorithm-racial-disparities-pymetrics-stanford-study/?rand=8593)

### Inferences
- PRAMAAN (e), (f) and (n) appear novel in the campus/TPO category. Versioned declarative rules with what-if and four-fifths preview before publishing, plus appeals, are absent from all sources. The global analogue (LL144 audits) is retrospective and annual, applied to vendor models, not prospective and officer-facing.
- In India there is no LL144-style mandate, so fairness tooling is a differentiator rather than a compliance requirement. Superset's "diversity preferences" criterion shows that demographic-aware filtering exists, which raises the stakes for fairness preview.

### Gaps
- Superset's actual eligibility UI and docs (rule history, audit log) could not be verified.
- No search covered Symplicity/12twenty eligibility (US career services rarely gate by eligibility rules).

## Q5. Which issue verifiable credentials or integrate with DigiLocker?

### Takeaway
Verifiable-credential infrastructure exists (Credly supports Open Badges 3.0; LinkedIn has usage-verified skills; India has DigiLocker/NAD/APAAR and a DGT skill-credential standard based on Open Badges v2). - India DGT Electronic Skill Credential Standard v1.0 extends Open Badges v2 (inCredible spec) and lists DigiLocker as a delivery channel — [BharatSkills ESCS PDF](https://www.bharatskills.gov.in/pdf/ESCS/Electronic_Skill_Credential_Standard_v1.0.pdf)
- DigiLocker–NAD integration lets institutions issue verified certificates; APAAR/ABC credit records are accessible via DigiLocker — [Inc42 APAAR](https://inc42.com/features/decoding-apaar-id-indias-new-aadhaar-like-student-id-system-to-secure-verify-academic-credits/); [PolicyEdge](https://www.policyedge.in/p/india-builds-education-digital-public-infrastructure-around-portable-academic-records)

### Gaps
- Coursera/edX OB 3.0 status not retrieved.
- Whether any Indian placement vendor (e.g. TruScholar-type partners) issues placement-linked skill badges was not confirmed.
- Credly's 1EdTech certification listing was not checked directly.
