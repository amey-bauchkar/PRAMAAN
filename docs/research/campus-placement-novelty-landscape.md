# Proof and recourse, not AI interviews, set PRAMAAN apart

A review of roughly 35 placement, assessment, talent-intelligence and credential platforms, plus the research and regulation around them, found that PRAMAAN's strongest claims to novelty are in **verification, recourse and fairness governance**. They are not in automation. The campus operational loop is already sold in India as standard: student data, rule-based eligibility filters, drives, assessments, proctoring, AI mock interviews, offers and NAAC/NIRF reports. Superset alone claims 1,400+ institutions, and in 2026 HackerRank, HireVue, HackerEarth and Unstop all shipped autonomous AI interviewers. None of the sources reviewed shows any of the following:

- a probe that makes a student predict, then change, the behaviour of their **own** repository under hidden tests;
- a check of an eligibility rule's fairness before it is published, or a suggested minimal fix to the rule;
- exact "smallest proof that unlocks the most drives" recourse;
- eligibility credentials that disclose only predicates, so recruiters never see raw marks or caste category;
- versioned eligibility rules with student appeals;
- college-signed, evidence-anchored skill credentials with proof levels and expiry.

The timing helps. Most DPDP duties start on 13 May 2027. EU AI Act high-risk duties for hiring and education start on 2 Dec 2027. Colorado's explanation duty starts on 1 Jan 2027. Vendor data puts flagged cheating at **48% in technical AI interviews**. Every "not found" in this report is absence of evidence, not proof of absence. The sweeps were time-boxed, relied mostly on vendor marketing pages, did not include patents, and could not see product documentation behind demos and sales calls. Claims should therefore read "no public prior art found", never "first ever".

## The drive loop and AI interviews are already commodity

Indian TPO software has settled on a common bundle. Superset's TnpSuite covers "student data and eligibility, job postings, applications, shortlists, interview rounds, and offers". Its Launchpad+ layer adds InterviewEdge AI mock interviews, the AVA conversational AI interviewer, TalentLens resume shortlisting and proctoring with "AI integrity checks". Superset claims 30M+ students and 1,400+ institutions ([Superset](https://joinsuperset.com/)). Its eligibility engine already handles CGPA cut-offs, backlogs, skills, assessment thresholds and "diversity preferences". Students "can see exactly which criteria they met or missed" ([Superset blog](https://joinsuperset.com/blogs/intelligent-eligibility-matching-using-a-placement-portal-to-align-student-profiles-with-recruiter-requirements/)). Basic eligibility transparency is therefore table stakes, not a differentiator. PrepInsta Optimus claims 100+ colleges ([CXOToday](https://cxotoday.com/media-coverage/prepinsta-joins-forces-with-100-educational-institutes-to-drive-student-upskilling-and-placement-success-across-india/)). Apna's resume-personalised mock interviews passed **7.6 lakh sessions** ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/news/apna-ai-job-prep-records-39-lakh-minutes-across-76-lakh-interview-simulations-9374546)). On 27 July 2026 Unstop launched seven hiring agents, including a Scheduling agent that "reads calendars" ([Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/unstop-launches-seven-ai-agents-to-automate-end-to-end-enterprise-hiring/)).

Accreditation reporting is also crowded. College ERPs advertise it directly: OpenEduCat has a "Generate SSR" button for NAAC and feeds NIRF submissions ([OpenEduCat](https://openeducat.org/college-management-software-in-india/)), MasterSoft produces NAAC/NBA/NIRF reports ([IITMS](https://iitms.co.in/products/accreditation-data-management-system)), and PI-360 claims 80% faster report generation ([PI-360](https://pi360.net)). The underlying need is real. NAAC metric 5.2.1 requires placed-student lists and offer letters, and data validation often asks for every letter ([BLDEA CET DVV](https://www.bldeacet.ac.in/assets/documents/dvv/criteria-5/5-2-1/5-2-1.pdf)). NIRF gives Graduation Outcomes a 0.20 weight in engineering ([NIRF framework](https://nirfindia.org/nirfpdfcdn/2024/framework/Engineering.pdf)). PRAMAAN should export this evidence because it drives adoption, but it should not present the export as innovation.

There is a demand gap behind all this tooling. HirePro's 2026 survey found only **9% of corporates think freshers are job-ready on day one**, against 68% of students who think they are ([HirePro](https://hirepro.in/resources/news/only-9-of-corporates-say-fresh-college-graduates-are-job-ready-on-day-1-while-68-of-students-believe-they-are-hirepro-report)). It also found 51% still let CGPA influence hiring, while only 21% have fully adopted skills-first hiring ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/jobs/story/skills-over-degrees-is-indias-hiring-system-ready-to-make-the-shift-544378-2026-07-22)). Employers distrust grades and use them anyway because they have no trusted alternative signal. Assessed employability is 56.35% overall but only 32.92% for polytechnic graduates ([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/india-skills-report-2026-cs-computer-science-it-engineer-top-jobs-mba-employment-decline-ai-ml-bfsi-fmcg-pharma-women-hiring)). That gap is the case for evidence over transcripts, especially outside elite CS programmes.

## 2026's AI interviewers probe vendor code, not the student's own

The AI-era verification market has shifted from detecting AI use to watching how candidates reason with it. HackerRank's Chakra reached general availability on 5 October 2026 after more than **500,000 interviews**. Candidates work in "a real-world code repository" supplied by HackerRank and explain "why they chose one approach over another" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/hackerranks-ai-interviewer-offers-a-glimpse-into-what-job-interviews-could-become/)). Karat NextGen has engineers probe candidates' reasoning on multi-file projects that Karat supplies ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251210685922/en)). CodeSignal records transcripts of every interaction with its in-IDE assistant ([CodeSignal](https://codesignal.com/newsroom/press-releases/codesignal-launches-ai-assisted-coding-assessments-and-interviews-redefining-technical-hiring-in-the-ai-era/)). Canva now expects candidates to use Copilot, Cursor or Claude, on the grounds that real engineering is mostly reading and owning code ([Canva](https://www.canva.dev/blog/engineering/yes-you-can-use-ai-in-our-interviews/)). In every case the artefact belongs to the vendor. None of these systems tests whether a student understands the code they claim as their own.

Integrity pressure is worst in exactly PRAMAAN's segment. Fabric analysed 19,368 AI-run interviews. It flagged 38.5% of candidates overall and **48% for technical roles**, with junior candidates flagged at about twice the senior rate ([Fabric](https://fabrichq.ai/blogs/state-of-ai-interview-cheating-in-2026-insights-from-19-368-interviews)). These are vendor probability flags with no disclosed false-positive rate. By contrast, only 6% of candidates in Gartner's survey admitted to fraud ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-07-31-gartner-survey-shows-just-26-percent-of-job-applicants-trust-ai-will-fairly-evaluate-them)), so true prevalence is unknown. In India, Infosys deferred assessments for over 20,000 applicants after detecting impersonation ([BW People](https://www.bwpeople.in/article/infosys-pauses-hiring-tests-amid-cheating-probe-610167)). Detection is not a safe answer. Liang et al. showed that GPT detectors misclassify non-native English writing at high rates and warned against using them in evaluation ([Patterns](https://www.cell.com/patterns/fulltext/S2666-3899(23)00130-7)). Code detectors in one evaluation collapsed to labelling nearly everything as AI, with F1 = 0.00 ([arXiv 2610.01664](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.01664)). Vanderbilt disabled Turnitin's AI detector ([Vanderbilt](https://www.vanderbilt.edu/brightspace/2023/08/16/guidance-on-ai-detection-and-why-were-disabling-turnitins-ai-detector/)). For a largely non-native-English Indian cohort, detector scores are a bias liability. They should route a candidate to a verification step, never decide an outcome.

Computing-education research supports the alternative. Work on "Questions about Learners' Code" shows that many students who submit working code cannot answer questions about it. In earlier work, about a third struggled to explain their own code ([arXiv 2306.16267](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16267)). GenAI-generated comprehension questions on students' own code are now peer-reviewed ([ACM 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3754508.3754532)). UWE Bristol made a code viva worth 30% of a first-year module ([UWE](https://digitallearning.uwe.ac.uk/ai-proofing-assessment-through-code-vivas/)). Mutation-based "did your summary track the behaviour change" probes are used only to test LLMs, which reached just **49.3%** accuracy on them ([arXiv 2602.17838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17838)). Open-source tools such as Code Defense and GitReady already turn a repo into interview questions ([Code Defense](https://gh.r-mj.com/AsadullahSamo/Code-Defense); [GitReady](https://lablab.ai/ai-hackathons/ibm-bob-hackathon/zayn/gitready)), so question generation alone is easy to copy. The defensible part is the combination: the student's own repo, system-generated mutations, a prediction made before execution, hidden-test scoring, and a link to proof levels and eligibility.

## India's credential rails carry whole documents, not predicates

India's education digital public infrastructure (DPI) is large but disclosure-heavy. NAD holds more than **110 crore academic records from about 3,420 awarding bodies**, and APAAR has 26.35 crore verified IDs ([PIB, Jul 2026](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/jul/doc202677915101.pdf); [PIB factsheet](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/jul/doc202675912501.pdf)). Employers verify records only after the student consents in DigiLocker, and they receive the whole document ([NAD FAQ](https://nad.digilocker.gov.in/faq)). A private platform can realistically be a DigiLocker *requester*, never an issuer. Requester partners "must be onboarded by MeitY" ([DigiLocker Partner API v2.2](https://cf-media.api-setu.in/resources/DigitalLocker-AuthorizedPartnerAPI-Specificationv2.2.pdf)), and a secondary source reports no sandbox and an India-hosting requirement ([Didit](https://didit.me/blog/digilocker-api/)). An MVP should mock DigiLocker behind an adapter. APAAR is Aadhaar-linked and legally contested ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/education/1640770/introduction-of-apaar-id-a-growing-privacy-concern)), so PRAMAAN should store verification outcomes and hashes, not the identifiers themselves. The government's skill-credential standard (DGT ESCS v1.0) extends **Open Badges v2**, not v3, and lists DigiLocker as a delivery channel ([BharatSkills ESCS](https://www.bharatskills.gov.in/pdf/ESCS/Electronic_Skill_Credential_Standard_v1.0.pdf)). A mapping layer is therefore needed.

The global standards are ready for skill credentials but not for zero-knowledge proofs. W3C VC Data Model 2.0 became a Recommendation on 15 May 2025. The BBS unlinkable-disclosure cryptosuite is still a Candidate Recommendation Draft ([W3C VC Overview](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/NOTE-vc-overview-20260617/)). Open Badges 3.0 was finalised in 2024 on top of W3C VCs ([1EdTech](https://www.1edtech.org/1edtech-article/new-open-badges-30-standard-provides-enhanced-security-and-mobility/411060)), and Credly supports it ([Credly](https://learn.credly.com/blog/credly-supports-open-badge-3.0)). SD-JWT is a finished standard, **RFC 9901 (Nov 2025)** ([RFC Editor](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9901.html)), with Apache-2.0 TypeScript packages under `@sd-jwt/*` ([sd-jwt-js](https://github.com/cre8/sd-jwt-js)). The SD-JWT VC profile is still an Internet-Draft in IESG evaluation ([IETF](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-oauth-sd-jwt-vc/)). Even the EU's flagship age-verification zero-knowledge design was not live in production builds as of August 2026 ([EFF](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/08/zkps-arent-age-verification-silver-bullets)). Indian eligibility rules combine Class 10, Class 12, every semester, backlogs and gap years ([placementpapers.app](https://placementpapers.app/eligibility-criteria/)). Issuer-computed boolean predicates such as `meets_cgpa_7: true`, selectively disclosed with SD-JWT, are the pragmatic design. Range proofs are not.

On the skill side, LinkedIn's Verified Skills (January 2026) verify tool proficiency from partner product usage such as Replit ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/28/linkedin-will-let-you-show-off-your-vibe-coding-chops-with-a-certificate)). Handshake's "AI Fellow" badges are platform badges based on participation, not verifiable credentials ([Handshake](https://updates.joinhandshake.com/announcements/ann_qIT5uQQpqhmOL)). No source showed a college issuing evidence-linked skill credentials in a placement context. The consent layer is also open. Consent Manager registration under the DPDP Rules opened only on 13 Nov 2026 ([dpdprules.org](https://dpdprules.org/rules/4)), and no Account-Aggregator-style consent framework for education data was found.

## Fairness tooling exists only as retrospective audits and papers

Outside India, fairness in hiring means **annual, model-level audits**. Eightfold's NYC Local Law 144 audit (March 2026) reports four-fifths impact ratios ([Eightfold](https://eightfold.ai/trust/bias-audit-results/)), and Harver publishes a similar audit ([Harver](https://harver.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Harver-Platform-2025-Bias-Audit.pdf)). Neither gives a placement officer a view of a specific drive before it goes live. Enforcement is weak: the New York State Comptroller found DCWP's LL144 enforcement "ineffective" ([NY OSC](https://www.osc.ny.gov/state-agencies/audits/2025/12/02/enforcement-local-law-144-automated-employment-decision-tools)). Litigation risk is rising. Eightfold faces a January 2026 class action alleging "secret candidate scoring" ([HR Executive](https://hrexecutive.com/as-eightfold-workday-suits-show-ai-legal-risks-are-building-for-hr/)), and a Stanford study found adverse impact on Black applicants in 10.62% of pymetrics-scored jobs ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/26/ai-hiring-algorithm-racial-disparities-pymetrics-stanford-study/?rand=8593)). PRAMAAN's "no hidden score, reason codes only" stance answers that controversy directly.

The regulatory calendar now clusters in 2027:

- **Colorado SB 26-189** takes effect 1 Jan 2027. After an adverse outcome it requires a plain-language explanation within 30 days, plus data correction and human review ([McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/colorado-ai-law-in-flux-comprehensive-replacement-bill-signed-after-federal-court-blocks-predecessors-enforcement/)).
- **DPDP Rules:** most substantive duties apply from 13 May 2027 ([dComply](https://dpdpa.dcomply.in/rules/)). DPDP has no right to an explanation of automated decisions. Its closest hook is the s.8(3) duty to keep decision-relevant data accurate, and that clause was not re-verified in this research ([MeitY](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)).
- **EU AI Act:** the Digital Omnibus moved Annex III high-risk obligations for employment and education to 2 Dec 2027. It also widened the legal basis for processing special-category data to detect bias ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)).

Shipping reason codes, correction, appeals and impact reporting by default would go beyond any Indian requirement. It would also make PRAMAAN ready for multinational recruiters.

The statistics and recourse literature show the build is tractable and point to its traps. With the small samples typical of a single drive, the four-fifths rule often flags adverse impact even when population selection rates are equal ([Roth, Bobko & Switzer 2006](https://web.pdx.edu/~mccunee/quant_621/Outlines/Roth,%20Bobko,%20Switzer%202006.doc)). Fisher's exact test is preferred for small cells ([Fulcrum](https://www.fulcrum.com/chi-square/)). A credible preview therefore pairs impact ratios with confidence intervals, exact tests and pooling across drives. Recourse research defines the goal as the minimal *feasible* change that flips a decision ([Verma et al.](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2010.10596)) and separates real improvement from gaming ([Miller, Milli & Hardt](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/miller20b.html)). It also shows that model-based recourse breaks under distribution shift: in one education study, following the recommendations improved grades for only **60.6%** of students ([arXiv 2505.14377](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14377v1)). Placement eligibility avoids that failure. Its rules are conjunctive thresholds, so exact recourse can be computed with no black box. In India, Western group definitions do not transfer cleanly ([Sambasivan et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.03659.pdf)). Caste can also be reconstructed from proxies even when surnames are removed ([The India Forum](https://www.theindiaforum.in/caste/algorithms-dont-need-your-surname-guess-your-caste-thats-problem)). Fairness audits therefore need to work without exposing category data. No commercial product was found doing publish-time fairness simulation, rule repair or formal recourse for campus drives.

## Ten candidates, ranked by novelty, defensibility and buildability

Each candidate is ranked by four things: how strongly the evidence suggests nobody offers it, how hard it is to copy, how directly it serves the QUASAR 5.0 brief, and whether a small team can ship it. The top six combine absence across every relevant sweep with low build risk or high strategic value. Ranks 9 and 10 are worth building, but competitors approach them, so they should be pitched as governance stances rather than inventions.

| 6 | **Anchored proof ladder** issued as college-signed OB 3.0 with expiry | Recruiter-side GitHub mining; Credly OB3; LinkedIn usage-verified skills | Medium-high | Medium: OB3 signing, ESCS v2 / NSQF mapping |

| 10 | **DEPA-style consent artefacts** per recruiter and drive | DigiLocker NAD consent; AA pattern in finance | Medium | Easy |

**The Live Mutation Probe ranks first** because it attacks the problem every 2026 AI interviewer avoids: whether this student understands the evidence they submitted. Each piece has precedent. Comprehension questions on one's own code ([ACM 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3754508.3754532)), "predict the output" orals ([Frontiers 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/artificial-intelligence/articles/10.3389/frai.2026.1945247/full)), LLM-generated viva questions ([TAMIGO](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16805)) and the informal "make a live change to your take-home" habit ([InterviewerSignal](https://interviewersignal.com/blog/how-to-verify-take-home-assignment-authorship)) all exist. What was not found anywhere is the combination: a multi-file own repo, generated semantic mutations, a prediction step, hidden-test scoring and a "why" follow-up. The prediction step is the strongest anti-AI element, given LLMs' weak tracking of mutated behaviour ([arXiv 2602.17838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17838)). Two limits must be stated. A candidate with an overlay tool can still query an LLM, so time limits and identity binding are separate, necessary layers. The probe also verifies *current understanding and ownership*, not original authorship. Feasibility is medium: an MVP needs one language (JS or Python), AST-level mutation operators, a sandboxed test runner and an LLM to draft the follow-up questions. No patent search was done, which is the largest open risk to this claim.

**Fairness simulation with rule repair ranks second** because it is novel and cheap. LL144-style audits are retrospective, annual and run on vendor models. No source showed an officer-facing preview before a drive is published. The search space is tiny: a few clauses and a few thousand students. The platform can therefore enumerate facially neutral edits, for example "lowering the CGPA cut-off from 7.5 to 7.2 raises the impact ratio from 0.71 to 0.86 and adds 23 eligible students" (an illustrative example, not real data). It should report Fisher p-values, confidence intervals and a shortfall count in people, which is easier for an officer to act on than a ratio. The legal anchor is the US "less discriminatory alternative" doctrine ([42 U.S.C. 2000e-2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/2000e-2)). The guardrail is equally clear: never use group-specific thresholds.

**The recourse engine ranks third.** Superset tells students which criteria they missed. No platform was found that computes the smallest *verified* change that unlocks the most upcoming drives. None classifies each failed clause as immutable (Class 10 marks), achievable before the drive deadline (clearing a backlog, uploading a certificate) or achievable for later drives (a required semester GPA). None ranks cohort interventions for TPOs by drives unlocked. Recourse should be offered only on verified fields, to avoid the gaming Miller et al. warn about. Freezing criteria at publish time guarantees recourse stability, which is the fix for the invalidation problem in the education study. The pitch should lead with "drives unlocked per proof", not "skill gaps", which is a crowded framing.

**Predicate-only eligibility credentials rank fourth.** No product, paper or pilot was found that implements selective-disclosure eligibility for Indian campus drives. In this model a recruiter sees `eligible: true` per criterion, signed by the college, and never sees raw marks, caste, gender or disability. Category data matters mainly for public-sector drives, and withholding it from private recruiters is a strong DPDP data-minimisation position. RFC 9901 and the `@sd-jwt/*` libraries make this buildable today. The caveat is that the SD-JWT VC profile is still a draft, and true zero-knowledge range proofs should not be promised.

**Versioned rules with reason codes and appeals rank fifth.** No platform documents rule history, what-if previews or a student appeals workflow. The nearest artefact is a manual SOP to "reevaluate students who improve eligibility" ([Zollege](https://knowledge.zollege.com/sop-internal/placement-support-eligibility-review)). Per-decision reason codes in the student's language would meet the strictest common standard across EU Art. 86, Colorado SB 26-189 and the ICO's recommendations ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2024/11/ico-intervention-into-ai-recruitment-tools-leads-to-better-data-protection-for-job-seekers)). Bhashini's free translation, speech-recognition and text-to-speech APIs ([Bhashini](https://bhashini.gitbook.io/bhashini-apis/)) make the language part cheap, though a fallback provider is needed. Every appeal should route to a named officer, and every override should be logged with a reason. Engineering effort is low and the trust payoff is high.

**The anchored proof ladder ranks sixth** because its parts are partly contested. Recruiter-side tools already mine GitHub. Prog.ai "analyzes each commit" ([Yahoo/TechCrunch](https://uk.news.yahoo.com/prog-ai-wants-help-recruiters-153923861.html)), and Mercor reportedly ingests GitHub for matching. These tools produce inferred profiles for search, without student consent, code-line anchors, proof levels or college issuance. Bundling anchored evidence, tiered proof levels (self-claimed, evidence-linked, probe-verified) and expiry into a college-signed OB 3.0 credential fills a gap that none of Credly, LinkedIn or Handshake covers. A v2 or ESCS mapping and NSQF-level tagging would allow later Indian government interoperability. Expiry is reasonable in principle, but the common "2.5-year skill half-life" figure is an unverified industry heuristic ([The Interview Guys](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/your-resume-has-a-half-life-now-why-the-skill-that-got-you-hired/)). Re-probe intervals should be policy choices, not presented as science.

**Contribution-scoped probes rank seventh.** Research on attribution in team repos uses git logs, `git blame` and LLM commit summaries. Its own authors warn that these are "proxies for student effort that do not necessarily indicate meaningful contribution" ([TRACE, arXiv 2510.03998](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03998v3)). Scoping the mutation probe to the files each member claims turns an activity signal into an understanding signal. No prior art was found, but the feature depends on #1 and matters mainly for group capstones.

**Fairness without exposure ranks eighth.** Per-funnel-stage monitoring (eligibility, then assessment, then AI or human interview, then offer) uses category data the college already holds for AISHE and scholarships. It enforces minimum-cell suppression and never exposes category to recruiters or uses it as a feature. The audit can be extended to less sensitive proxies such as rural origin, medium of schooling and first-generation status. The idea is sound, and the EU Omnibus's bias-detection basis gives an international precedent. Data governance with colleges, not code, is the hard part.

**Ranks nine and ten are useful positions rather than inventions.** Agentic workflows are everywhere in 2026, and Unstop's agents describe no human approval gate. A "proposal-only, officer-approves, everything logged" design, for example turning a company's email into a proposed drive schedule, is a governance differentiator that HireVue and HackerRank partly match. Likewise, purpose-bound, time-bound, revocable consent records per recruiter and drive, modelled on the Account Aggregator pattern, set PRAMAAN up to work with registered Consent Managers later. They are not new mechanisms.

Two pitched items stay unranked. Multi-college tenancy with university roll-up is described publicly almost nowhere. That is unknowable from outside, not evidence of a gap. India-grounded skill-graph matching is a genuine research gap: ESCO/O*NET dominate ([ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.genaik-1.15)), and placement-prediction papers rely on Kaggle-scale datasets of a few hundred rows ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/9/11/1166/xml)). But it is a data asset that takes years to build, not a hackathon-demonstrable feature.

## Conclusion

The incumbents are racing on automation. More agents and more AI interviewers make the hiring funnel faster, but they do not make it more trustworthy. Integrity failures (48% flagged technical interviews, the Infosys deferrals) and opaque scoring (the Eightfold lawsuit) are now the category's main liabilities. PRAMAAN's opening is to be the **trust layer around a commodity loop**. That means proofs a student can defend live, rules an officer can test for fairness before publishing, recourse a student can act on, and credentials that tell a recruiter only what it needs to know. These features reinforce each other: verified evidence makes recourse resistant to gaming, frozen versioned rules make recourse stable, and predicate credentials let fairness audits run without exposing caste. Copying one feature is easy. Copying the system is not.

Two strategic implications follow. First, the 2027 cluster of compliance dates (Colorado in January, DPDP in May, the EU in December) turns these features from ethics talking points into procurement criteria for multinational recruiters during the 2026–27 placement season. PRAMAAN should demo the reason-code and appeals trail, not the mock interviewer. Second, India's DPI favours **attestation over cryptography**. NAD and DigiLocker give PRAMAAN an authoritative, consented source of marks, and college-signed boolean predicates are the realistic privacy upgrade. Zero-knowledge proofs are a roadmap item at most. Before any public novelty claim, the team should run a patent search on repository-derived assessment and get demo-level access to Superset's eligibility module, since those are the two places most likely to hide prior art this research could not see.
